\section{Introduction}

Stock-price forecasting asks a simple question: how much of a future price can past observations explain? For price-level prediction, the latest observed close already provides a forecast without model training. This rule, known as latest-close persistence, is a standard naive forecasting method \cite{hyndman2018forecasting}. A learned model must improve on this comparator before its lower error against another learned model establishes an advantage over persistence.

The target and evaluation design determine what such an advantage means. Predicting a future price level differs from predicting its direction, an excess return, or a profitable trading decision. A model and its baseline also need the same forecast origins and target dates for a meaningful error comparison. Different feature requirements can otherwise change the usable observations and the resulting test period.

This study asks whether selected neural and zero-shot time-series models beat persistence in cross-market price-level prediction. The experiment grid covers 165 asset entries, representing 133 unique tickers across five configured lists. It includes two price modes and three forecast horizons, with several compact supervised implementations and Chronos-T5-small. Only 39 of 35,640 supervised configuration--seed rows achieve lower RMSE than matched persistence, and every supervised implementation has a median RMSE ratio above one. Chronos has local wins, but its sampled-origin median also remains above one; its different protocol prevents a direct ranking against the supervised models.

The paper makes three contributions:
\begin{itemize}
\item A baseline-first evaluation that pairs each model forecast with persistence on the same origins and target dates.
\item A cross-market comparison of selected compact implementations and one small zero-shot model, with separate reporting for different evaluation protocols.
\item Corrected evaluation records with dated forecasts, explicit experiment keys, and descriptive summaries that make the scope of the negative result clear.
\end{itemize}

The following sections review related work, describe the corrected protocol, and report the results. The discussion and limitations explain which conclusions the experiment supports. The reproducibility sections distinguish the corrected local evidence from the earlier public archive.
