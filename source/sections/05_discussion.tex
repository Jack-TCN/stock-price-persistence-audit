\section{Discussion}

The corrected results support a limited conclusion: most configurations in this experiment do not improve on matched persistence. The interpretation depends on the price-level target, the compact implementations, and the evaluation protocol. The following discussion explains the role of the baseline and the boundaries of the evidence.

\subsection{The role of persistence}

The persistence forecast starts from the current price level and assumes no change over the forecast horizon. A learned model must estimate a level accurately enough to improve on that simple rule. Lag or smoothing errors may help explain why a visually plausible prediction still has higher RMSE. The present experiment does not isolate those mechanisms, so they remain possible explanations rather than demonstrated causes.

The rare wins also need a precise interpretation. The 39 supervised RMSE wins are configuration--seed rows, not 39 independent assets. Twenty rows improve all three error measures, which is a stricter descriptive criterion. Neither count establishes a generally superior forecasting system, because related rows reuse assets, targets, and observations.

\subsection{The scope of the negative result}

Every supervised implementation has a median RMSE ratio above one, and Chronos-T5-small also has a median above one within its sampled-origin protocol. These summaries describe the fixed experiment grid. They do not identify the performance of official full-capacity architectures, larger foundation models, or alternative training searches. The different origins, context lengths, and input variables also prevent a direct model-family ranking.

The target further limits the conclusion. The study evaluates future absolute closing prices rather than returns, direction, volatility, portfolio rankings, or trading profit. Those tasks need their own loss functions, benchmarks, and evaluation designs. The result therefore supports caution about forecasting claims for this price-level task, rather than a claim that financial forecasting is impossible.

\subsection{Implications for evaluation}

For price-level studies, we recommend reporting persistence alongside learned-model comparisons. Each comparison should state its forecast origins, target dates, training information, and checkpoint-selection rule. A claimed gain from indicators or architecture also needs a design that holds the relevant evaluation conditions constant. When gains occur only in particular assets, horizons, or seeds, the conclusion should retain those conditions.

The correction also shows why dated forecasts matter. An error ratio can be arithmetically correct while comparing model and baseline errors from different test samples. The corrected workflow stores both forecasts on the same dates and verifies their metrics against the original price records. This improves traceability, but it does not remove the remaining data-availability, selection, and statistical-dependence limits.
