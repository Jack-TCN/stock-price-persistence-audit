\section{Results}

The corrected results compare each model with persistence on the same forecast origins and target dates. Table~\ref{tab:model_gate_summary} summarizes the RMSE gate and the separate all-three criterion. Table~\ref{tab:horizon_summary} reports horizon-specific medians. We discuss supervised and zero-shot results separately because their inputs and test-origin protocols differ.

\input{tables/crossmarket_core_tables}

\subsection{Supervised results}

Only 39 of 35,640 supervised configuration--seed rows pass the RMSE gate, a rate of 0.11\%. LSTM contributes six wins, GRU 21, the linear temporal proxy none, PatchTST-style two, iTransformer-style three, and TimeMixer-style seven. Twenty rows improve all three error measures. Every supervised implementation has a median RMSE ratio above one, ranging from 1.92 for GRU to 3.53 for iTransformer-style. Under this protocol, persistence therefore has lower error than most of the selected learned configurations.

The horizon summaries also show median ratios above one for every supervised implementation at 1, 5, and 10 days. None of the supervised rows passes at one day, while nine pass at five days and 30 at ten days. The 39 winning rows involve nine unique tickers and 29 distinct list--asset--price--horizon--feature--implementation conditions before seeds. Only one such condition passes with all three seeds: GRU on adjusted TLT prices, a ten-day horizon, and OHLCV inputs. These counts describe local results in the fixed grid rather than independent evidence of a general forecasting advantage.

\subsection{Chronos results}

Chronos-T5-small passes in 119 of 990 sampled-origin rows, or 12.02\%, and improves all three errors in 67 rows. Its median RMSE ratio is 1.036, so the median row remains slightly worse than persistence. Individual wins occur in several asset lists. The nominal win rate does not rank Chronos against the supervised models, because the contexts, variables, and forecast origins differ. Figure~\ref{fig:rmse_ratio_distribution} shows the descriptive distributions on separate scales.

The full-origin Chronos subset has one RMSE win among 60 rows and one all-three win. Its median ratio is 1.032, also above one. This subset contains the first two asset entries from each list, representing nine unique tickers. It provides a limited sensitivity check rather than a representative test of every asset. Separate generation in the two runs also prevents us from attributing their differences only to origin sampling.

\subsection{Patterns across lists}

Table~\ref{tab:market_gate} separates gate counts by asset list and protocol. Every supervised implementation has a median RMSE ratio above one within each list, although some configurations win. The frequency of local wins varies across lists. These summaries retain list memberships, so a stock that appears in several lists contributes several times. The pattern supports a bounded cross-market result for this selected universe, not a claim that every configuration or every financial forecasting model fails.
