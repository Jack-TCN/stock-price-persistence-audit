\begin{abstract}
This study compares selected neural and zero-shot time-series models with latest-close persistence for stock-price forecasting across markets. This simple baseline uses the latest observed closing price to predict future price levels. Daily data from 2015 to April 2026 cover stocks in the United States (US) and Hong Kong and a global exchange-traded fund (ETF) basket. The five lists contain 165 asset entries and 133 unique tickers. The experiments cover two price modes and three forecast horizons, with each model and its baseline sharing the same test origins and target dates. The supervised implementations include long short-term memory (LSTM), gated recurrent unit (GRU), a linear temporal proxy, and compact PatchTST-style, iTransformer-style, and TimeMixer-style models. Only 39 of 35,640 supervised configuration--seed rows achieve lower root mean squared error (RMSE) than persistence, and every implementation has a median RMSE ratio above one. Chronos-T5-small passes in 119 of 990 sampled-origin rows, with a median ratio of 1.036. Its different protocol prevents a direct ranking against the supervised models. These descriptive results support a bounded conclusion: most selected configurations do not improve on persistence for absolute price-level prediction under the tested protocols. The study provides a baseline-first evaluation with dated forecasts and explicit limits on cross-model comparisons.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} stock forecasting; persistence baseline; time-series foundation models; Chronos; PatchTST; iTransformer; TimeMixer; negative results
